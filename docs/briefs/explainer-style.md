# Brief: the Illustrated explainer style

## Goal

A diagram style for explaining Knowledge Islands: architecture posters, explainers, decks and the Field Guide's "How the map works" page. It is a drawing style, not a tile set.

## Character

These two boards are style references only: they show how a diagram is drawn, not what it says. Their subjects, gstack and an agentic operating system, are not part of this brief.

From `references/explainer-how-gstack-works.png` and `explainer-agentic-operating-system.png`:

- Daylight: watercolour islands on a pale turquoise sea, soft cloud and foam, warm parchment ground.
- Information sits in parchment cartouches with thin rules and rounded corners; headings in a strong geometric or classical capital, body in a clean sans.
- Each concept has a coloured line icon inside a roundel; one hue per role, used consistently down a column.
- Flows are dashed routes with small arrowheads; the iteration loop sits at the foot as "the tide", a row of six numbered roundels joined by dashes, looping back.
- A lighthouse or compass anchors the top-left; a small brand lockup sits bottom-right.

## Rules

- Concepts and names come from the [concept atlas](concept-atlas.md); never invent a meaning to fill a box.
- Agents are workers on islands, not islands themselves: knowledge and work are the subject. Where a poster shows agent roles, map them to the steward roles (Steward, Archivist, Cartographer, Builder, Navigator, Scout, Historian, Guardian).
- Text 4.5:1 on its ground; no more than about 40 words in any cartouche.
- No humanoid robots, glowing AI eyes, neon, crystals or magic.

## First commission

"How the map works": a single poster in this style explaining hex topology → data layers → isometric render → final map, and how condition states (thriving, quiet, weathered, ruined) report evidence freshness. Base it on `references/explainer-hex-and-isometric.png`.
